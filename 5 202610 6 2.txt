Over the past week, Delaney Hall in Newark has erupted. Around 300 immigration detainees at the privately run GEO Group facility launched a coordinated hunger and work strike, telling families and lawyers that they’re being served food with live worms and maggots, drinking metallic-tasting water, packed into stifling, un‑air‑conditioned rooms, and denied basic medical treatment for chronic conditions.

They say immigration judges are skipping their cases or denying bond to pressure them into “self‑deporting.” This isn’t one or two complaints — it’s a pattern that’s been building for months and finally boiled over.

Outside, the protest has grown into an around‑the‑clock vigil: relatives, clergy, students, and local organizers camping out in the parking lot, holding signs with detainees’ names, chanting so people inside know they’re not alone. On Monday, when demonstrators blocked a bus carrying detainees, ICE and federal agents in riot gear charged.

Video and eyewitness accounts show them firing pepper balls and what appears to be tear gas into the crowd. New Jersey Senator Andy Kim, who came to monitor the protest and try to speak with ICE officials, ended up coughing and flushed with pepper spray alongside the activists he was there to support.

Inside the building, a small group of Democratic members of Congress were finally allowed to tour. What they described afterward was grim: small portions of food that “very often” contained maggots, detainees who said Tylenol was the only medication they could get, and widespread fear of retaliation for speaking up.
